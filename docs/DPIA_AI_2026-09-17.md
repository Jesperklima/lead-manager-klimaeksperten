# Lead Manager — DPIA / AI risk assessment baseline — 2026-09-17

Status: draft assessment requiring named owner review and sign-off. It is deliberately marked `required_review` in production governance.

## Processing covered

Lead Manager processes B2B company and business-contact information, source/provenance evidence, CRM activities, mail data when an integration is enabled, pipeline/offer data, public-source lead research, AI-assisted drafting/classification and customer-specific AI learning examples. It also links several systems and may score company credit risk using public CVR signals.

## Why a DPIA-style assessment is warranted

The product combines multiple sources, contact enrichment, decision-maker/relevance classification, communication histories, AI assistance and integrations. Even where each element is ordinary B2B CRM processing, the combination can increase risks of inaccurate profiling, excessive retention, unexpected use, cross-tenant disclosure and unlawful outreach.

## Design restrictions already required

- Human review is mandatory for AI-assisted outputs.
- AI may not send mail without an explicit user send/approval action.
- AI may not make decisions with legal or similarly significant effect on its own.
- Personal/natural-person credit scoring is prohibited.
- Sensitive personal data should not be placed in AI prompts as an ordinary workflow.
- Personally owned businesses require manual review in credit assessment rather than automatic risk scoring.
- Contact data without documented provenance is review-required and cannot be treated as verified automatically.
- Tenant access is protected by RLS/client-access functions.
- Closed/inactive prospects enter a retention review/anonymisation lifecycle rather than being stored indefinitely by default.

## Main risks and controls

### Incorrect or fabricated contact data
Risk: a wrong person, wrong source or inferred email leads to inappropriate contact or inaccurate CRM records.
Controls: no inferred-email workflow; source URL/provenance fields; provenance guard; verified flag downgrade; review queue; source-transparency UI.

### Unlawful direct marketing
Risk: GDPR storage basis is mistaken for permission to send marketing email.
Controls: channel/purpose permissions; direct-marketing approval guard; stoplist/do-not-contact; separate marketing-law logic; manual review for edge cases.

### Excessive retention
Risk: lost, closed or stale prospects and mail/AI examples remain indefinitely.
Controls: configurable retention settings, retention queue, quarantine, legal holds, anonymisation, daily retention cron, separate minimal suppression evidence.

### AI hallucination or over-reliance
Risk: AI draft/classification is inaccurate and treated as fact or sent automatically.
Controls: human approval; AI-labelled workflow; no autonomous send; source evidence for lead facts; AI policy and training.

### Automated financial judgement about natural persons
Risk: company tooling is extended to persons/sole proprietors and produces automated creditworthiness decisions.
Controls: governance flag `personal_credit_scoring_allowed=false`; entity-form check; manual review for personally owned businesses; company-only public CVR risk logic.

### Cross-tenant disclosure
Risk: one SaaS customer sees another customer's CRM/mail data.
Controls: Supabase RLS on CRM tables, client access function, tenant-scoped RPCs, platform-admin controls and audit requirements.

### External processors/transfers
Risk: data is sent to vendors without complete contractual/transfer documentation.
Controls: maintained subprocessor register, DPA status not marked complete until verified, vendor minimisation, scoped integrations and documented review.

### Gmail restricted-scope handling
Risk: external SaaS deployment processes restricted Gmail data without required Google verification/security evidence.
Controls: restricted-scope readiness checklist, least privilege, OAuth review, data deletion/retention controls; broad launch depends on external verification where applicable.

## Necessity and proportionality

Only data relevant to B2B sales/service workflows should be collected. Contact data must be traceable to a source. The system should prefer company-level information where a named person is unnecessary. Email bodies and AI learning examples are more intrusive than basic company data and therefore receive shorter/minimised retention and stricter access.

Outbound automation is intentionally constrained: the product can help draft and organise, but it should not turn a public-source lead into unsolicited electronic marketing without a separately documented permission.

## Residual risk requiring owner review

- Final retention periods must be justified for the actual customer/business model rather than copied mechanically.
- Article 14 workflow/exceptions need controller-specific assessment.
- DPA/subprocessor and transfer evidence must be completed externally.
- Support impersonation must remain time-limited, purpose-bound and logged.
- Google OAuth restricted-scope verification/security requirements must be completed before any rollout that requires them.
- New AI features that materially change profiling, autonomy or data categories trigger a fresh assessment.
