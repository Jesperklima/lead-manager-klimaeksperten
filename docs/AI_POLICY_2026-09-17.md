# Lead Manager AI policy and AI-literacy baseline — 2026-09-17

Policy version: `2026-09-17-v1`

## Core rule

AI is an assistant, not the accountable decision-maker. A human user remains responsible for factual review, appropriateness and any communication or business decision made from an AI output.

## Allowed uses

- Drafting and improving CRM email text for user review.
- Summarising CRM activities and mail threads.
- Classifying lead relevance using documented customer criteria.
- Extracting structured facts from documented sources where source/provenance remains visible.
- Suggesting follow-up wording, tasks or research areas.
- Company-level public CVR analysis where the output is clearly scoped to the available public signals and not represented as a full credit report.

## Prohibited uses

- Sending customer/prospect emails without explicit user approval/send action.
- Making legal or similarly significant decisions about a person without human decision-making and a separately approved lawful process.
- Producing or storing a personal/natural-person creditworthiness score.
- Treating a sole proprietorship/personally owned business as an ordinary limited company for automatic credit scoring.
- Inventing contact information, source URLs, consents, legal bases or verification evidence.
- Using sensitive/special-category personal data in prompts as a normal CRM feature.
- Circumventing a stoplist, objection, do-not-contact flag, channel block, retention hold or provenance requirement.
- Presenting probabilistic AI inference as verified fact.

## Required human checks

Before using AI-generated content externally, verify recipient, company, names, factual claims, source context, offer/price commitments, confidentiality, legal/marketing permission and tone. A send button is an approval action, not proof that the content is correct.

## Data minimisation for AI

Send the minimum context required for the task. Prefer company and role context over unnecessary personal histories. Do not include passwords, secrets, payment credentials, health data, political/religious information or other special-category data unless a specifically approved workflow and lawful basis require it.

Customer-specific AI learning examples must be retained only for the configured period and minimised/anonymised when the related prospect purpose expires.

## Transparency

The UI should label AI-assisted features as AI assistance and make clear when user review is required. Where a person directly interacts with an AI system in a context covered by AI transparency obligations, the product owner must verify and implement the specific disclosure requirement before enabling that feature.

## AI literacy checklist

Staff and customer administrators who use AI-assisted features should understand:

1. AI output can be wrong, incomplete or fabricated.
2. Source-backed facts are stronger than model assertions.
3. Personal data minimisation still applies when using AI.
4. GDPR legal basis and marketing-law permission are different questions.
5. A public email address is not automatically permission for cold marketing email.
6. Objections, stoplists and do-not-contact controls override sales goals.
7. Sole proprietors can involve natural-person rights and require extra care.
8. AI must not autonomously send mail or make legal-effect decisions in Lead Manager.
9. Suspected data leakage, wrong-recipient mail, cross-tenant access or harmful AI behaviour must be escalated as a compliance/security incident.
10. Material new AI features require renewed risk/DPIA review.

Training completion should be recorded by person/date/version rather than assumed from access to the product.

## Change control

Changes to model provider, model purpose, autonomy, data categories, recipient groups, credit logic or profiling behaviour require an AI governance review. The live `crm_ai_governance` settings are safety controls and must not be weakened by a UI-only change.
